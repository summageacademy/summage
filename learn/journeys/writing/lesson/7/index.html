How to write powerful introductions for Task 2

Hey! Let's talk about something that can make a huge difference in your Task 2 score now. Writing a powerful introduction! A strong introduction grabs the examiner's attention, shows that you understand the question, and clearly tells them where you stand. Many students write weak or copied introductions and lose valuable marks right from the start. I'll show you exactly how to do it properly.

Why Does the Introduction Matter So Much?

The introduction is the first thing the examiner reads. It creates the first impression. A good introduction helps you score higher in Task Response and Coherence. It should be around 40–60 words (2–3 sentences) and set up the whole essay.

The Perfect Formula for Task 2 Introductions

1
Paraphrase the question (1 sentence)
Rewrite the topic in your own words.
2
Give your opinion or outline your position (1 sentence)
Clearly state what you think or what the essay will discuss.

Pro Tip: Never copy the exact words from the question. Always paraphrase!

Real Examples

Question: Some people believe that the best way to increase road safety is to increase the minimum legal age for driving cars or riding motorbikes. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Weak Introduction:

Some people think the best way to increase road safety is to increase the minimum legal age for driving. I will discuss this.

Strong Introduction (Band 7.5+):

Road safety is a major concern in many countries today. While some people argue that raising the minimum legal age for drivers and motorbike riders is the most effective solution, I completely agree with this view because younger people tend to take more risks.

Useful Language for Introductions

Paraphrasing Starters:
It is often argued that… • Many people believe that… • In recent years, there has been growing concern about… • It is widely believed that…
Stating Your Position:
In my opinion… • I strongly believe that… • From my perspective… • This essay will argue that… • I completely agree / disagree with this statement because…

Common Mistakes Students Make

  • Copying the question directly
  • Making the introduction too long (more than 3 sentences)
  • Not clearly stating their opinion
  • Using very basic vocabulary (“Some people think… I think…”)
  • Writing general statements that don’t relate to the question
  • Forgetting to mention the topic properly

Practical Tips for Powerful Introductions

  1. Spend 2–3 minutes understanding the question before writing
  2. Identify the exact topic and the type of essay (opinion, discussion, problem/solution, etc.)
  3. Practice paraphrasing common IELTS topics
  4. Always include your clear position in the introduction
  5. Read your introduction after finishing the essay to make sure it matches what you wrote
  6. Keep it simple but impressive as clarity is more important than fancy words

A powerful introduction is like a strong foundation for your entire essay. Once you master this, the rest of the essay becomes much easier to write. Practice writing introductions for 10 different questions and you’ll notice a big improvement in your confidence and scores.
