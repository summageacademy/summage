Understanding IELTS Writing Task 1

Your Complete Friendly Guide to Academic Writing Task 1

Hey there! If you're preparing for IELTS, Writing Task 1 can feel a bit scary at first. Don't worry, I've been helping students with this for years, and I'm going to walk you through everything in a simple, practical way. By the end of this lesson, you'll know exactly what to do.

What Exactly Is Writing Task 1?

In IELTS Academic Writing Task 1, you get a visual (a chart, graph, table, map, or process diagram) and you have to describe the information in your own words. You need to write at least 150 words in about 20 minutes.

The goal isn't to give your opinion. Your job is to report and summarize the key information clearly and accurately. Think of yourself as a neutral reporter explaining data to someone who can't see the image.

IELTS Writing Task 1 Assessment Criteria

Examiners score your Task 1 on four things:

  • Task Achievement (25%) – Did you cover all key features? Is there a clear overview?
  • Coherence & Cohesion (25%) – Is your writing logical and well-organized?
  • Lexical Resource (25%) – Do you use a good range of vocabulary accurately?
  • Grammatical Range & Accuracy (25%) – Do you use varied sentence structures correctly?

Pro tip: Many students lose marks because they miss a proper overview or don't select the most important trends. Focus on these two things and you're already ahead of most candidates.

Common Task Types

📈 Line Graphs

Show changes over time. You talk about trends (increase, decrease, fluctuate, etc.).

📊 Bar Charts

Compare different categories or groups. Great for making comparisons.

🥧 Pie Charts

Show proportions and percentages. Usually multiple pies for comparison.

📋 Tables

Contain lots of numbers. You need to group and select key data smartly.

🗺️ Maps

Describe changes in a place over time (e.g., development of a town).

⚙️ Process Diagrams

Explain steps in a natural or manufacturing process (e.g., life cycle of a butterfly or making chocolate).

The Perfect Structure (Recommended)

1
Introduction (1-2 sentences)
Paraphrase the question. Tell the reader what the chart shows.
2
Overview (2-3 sentences)
This is the most important paragraph! Summarize the main trends or key features without specific numbers.
3
Body Paragraph 1
Describe the most noticeable features with data.
4
Body Paragraph 2
Describe the remaining details, make comparisons where relevant.

Useful Vocabulary & Phrases

Trends (Up)
increased sharply • rose steadily • climbed dramatically • surged • went up significantly
Trends (Down)
decreased • fell • dropped • declined • plummeted • slumped
No Change
remained stable • stayed constant • leveled off • plateaued
Comparisons
twice as much as • significantly higher than • the highest • the lowest • similar to

Sample Task & Answer

Task: The graph below shows the average monthly temperatures in two cities over a year.

The chart illustrates the average monthly temperatures in Sydney and London throughout the year. Overall, Sydney experiences much warmer weather than London. While temperatures in Sydney are highest during the summer months, London has its warmest period in July and August. In Sydney, temperatures start at around 23°C in January and gradually decrease to their lowest point of approximately 15°C in July. They then rise again towards the end of the year. In contrast, London has much cooler temperatures overall. The coldest months are January and February, with averages around 5-6°C. Temperatures then increase steadily, peaking at about 18°C in July and August before declining again.

Notice how the overview highlights the main difference (Sydney is warmer) without going into exact numbers. That's what makes a strong Task 1 response.

Common Mistakes Students Make

  • Writing less than 150 words
  • Missing the overview paragraph
  • Listing every single number instead of summarizing
  • Giving personal opinions ("I think this is bad for the environment")
  • Using the same vocabulary repeatedly
  • Not making comparisons when the data clearly shows them

Quick Tips for Success

  1. Spend 2-3 minutes studying the chart before writing
  2. Always identify the highest and lowest points
  3. Group similar information together
  4. Use a variety of sentence structures
  5. Practice writing one Task 1 every day
  6. Time yourself — 20 minutes only

Remember: Consistency beats perfection. The more you practice describing different types of visuals, the more confident you'll become.
