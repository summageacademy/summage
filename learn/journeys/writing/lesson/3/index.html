Writing an Effective Overview

Key techniques for writing high-scoring overviews

Hey! If there’s one thing that separates a Band 6 from a Band 7.5 or 8 in IELTS Writing Task 1, it’s usually the Overview. Many students either skip it or write it poorly, and that single paragraph can cost them a full band. Today I’m going to explain everything about writing an effective overview in a very practical, easy-to-follow way.

Why is the Overview So Important?

The overview is the second paragraph (after the introduction) and it gives the examiner a clear summary of the main trends or key features in the chart. It shows that you can see the “big picture” instead of just listing numbers.

According to official IELTS criteria, a clear overview is required for Band 7+ in Task Achievement. Without it, even if your English is perfect, it’s very hard to get above Band 6.5.

What Should an Overview Contain?

A strong overview should:

  • Summarize the most important trends or changes
  • Highlight the highest and lowest points (if relevant)
  • Show comparisons between categories or time periods
  • Be written in 2–3 sentences
  • NOT include specific numbers or data

Think of it like this: If someone can’t see the chart, your overview should give them the main story in simple words.

The Perfect Overview Formula

1
Start with “Overall,” — this is the most common and safe way to begin.
2
Mention the main trend(s) — what is happening overall?
3
Add one more key feature or comparison (highest/lowest, biggest change, etc.).

Real Examples

Task: The graph below shows the average monthly temperatures in Sydney and London.

Weak Overview (Band 5–6):

Overall, there are temperatures in both cities.

Strong Overview (Band 7.5+):

Overall, Sydney experiences significantly warmer weather than London throughout the year. While temperatures in Sydney peak during the summer months, London reaches its highest temperatures in July and August.

Another Example (Bar Chart):

Overall, it is clear that China consumed the most energy among all the countries, while Germany had the lowest consumption. Renewable energy sources made up the smallest proportion in all countries except Germany.

Useful Phrases for Overviews

Starting:
Overall, • It is clear that • It can be seen that • As can be seen from the chart,
Main Trends:
experienced a significant increase • saw a dramatic rise • remained relatively stable • fluctuated slightly
Comparisons:
was by far the highest • had the lowest figure • followed by • while … had …
Key Features:
reached a peak of • hit its lowest point • the biggest difference was between … and …

Common Mistakes Students Make

  • Skipping the overview completely
  • Putting specific numbers in the overview
  • Writing the overview at the end instead of after the introduction
  • Making it too short (only one sentence) or too long
  • Copying words directly from the question
  • Focusing on small details instead of the big picture

Pro Tips for Writing Strong Overviews

  1. Spend 1–2 minutes just looking at the chart and asking yourself: “What is the main story here?”
  2. Always look for the overall trend (up, down, stable, fluctuation) and the most noticeable differences
  3. Identify the highest and lowest points quickly
  4. Write the overview right after the introduction! Do NOT leave it for last!
  5. Practice writing overviews for 10 different charts without writing the full essay
  6. Read your overview and ask: “If I remove all numbers, does it still make sense?”

Mastering the overview is one of the fastest ways to improve your Task 1 score. It is not about being fancy but rather about showing the examiner that you truly understand the data.
