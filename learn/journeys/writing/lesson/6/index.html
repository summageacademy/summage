Understanding IELTS Writing Task 2

Your Complete Friendly Guide to Academic Writing Task 2

Hey! Now that we've covered Writing Task 1 in detail, it's time to talk about Writing Task 2. It is the bigger and more important part of the IELTS Writing test. This is where you can really show your English ability, and it's also where most students lose the most marks if they're not prepared. I'll walk you through everything you need to know in a straightforward, practical way.

What is IELTS Writing Task 2?

In Task 2, you have to write an essay responding to a given statement or question. You need to write at least 250 words in about 40 minutes.

Unlike Task 1, where you only describe data, Task 2 requires you to think, give opinions, explain ideas, and support them with reasons and examples. This task is worth twice as many marks as Task 1, so it's crucial to do well here.

Key Differences Between Task 1 and Task 2

  • Task 1 = Describe visual information (no opinion)
  • Task 2 = Give your opinion, discuss ideas, argue, or suggest solutions
  • Task 2 needs clear arguments and logical structure
  • You have more time (40 minutes) but also higher expectations

IELTS Writing Task 2 Assessment Criteria

Examiners score Task 2 on four areas:

  • Task Response (25%) – Did you fully answer the question? Are your ideas relevant and well-developed?
  • Coherence & Cohesion (25%) – Does your essay flow logically? Are ideas well connected?
  • Lexical Resource (25%) – Do you use a wide range of vocabulary accurately?
  • Grammatical Range & Accuracy (25%) – Do you use different sentence types correctly?

Important: Task Response is usually where students lose the most marks. Even if your English is good, if you don’t directly answer the question, your score will suffer.

Common Task Types in Task 2

Opinion Essays

"To what extent do you agree or disagree?" – You give your opinion and support it.

Discussion Essays

"Discuss both views and give your opinion." – You talk about two sides.

Problem / Solution Essays

You explain causes of a problem and suggest solutions.

Advantages / Disadvantages Essays

Discuss positive and negative sides of something.

Two-Part Questions

"Why is this happening? What can be done?" – Answer both questions.

The Standard Essay Structure

1
Introduction (40–60 words)
Paraphrase the question + clearly state your position/thesis.
2
Body Paragraph 1
Main idea + explanation + example.
3
Body Paragraph 2
Another main idea + explanation + example.
4
Conclusion (30–50 words)
Summarize your main points and restate your position.

Time Management Tips

  • 5 minutes → Planning (very important!)
  • 30–32 minutes → Writing
  • 3–5 minutes → Checking for mistakes

Always plan before writing. A good plan helps you stay on topic and saves time later.

Common Mistakes Students Make

  • Not answering the exact question (going off-topic)
  • Writing less than 250 words
  • Having no clear position or opinion
  • Repeating the same ideas or vocabulary
  • Using very short, simple sentences only
  • Forgetting to include examples to support ideas

Quick Tips to Score Higher in Task 2

  1. Always take a clear position and stick to it
  2. Support every main idea with a reason and a relevant example
  3. Use linking words naturally (however, moreover, for example, in addition)
  4. Show a range of vocabulary and grammar. Do NOT play it too safe!
  5. Practice writing full essays under timed conditions
  6. Read sample high-band essays regularly to see what good writing looks like

Task 2 is your opportunity to shine. It is mostly about clear thinking and good organization. The more you practice with real questions, the more natural it will feel.
