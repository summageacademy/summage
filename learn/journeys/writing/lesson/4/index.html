Body Paragraphs: Trends & Data

How to describe trends, numbers and comparisons

Hey! You've made it to one of the most important parts of Task 1, the body paragraphs. This is where you show the examiner that you can describe the chart in detail, talk about trends clearly, and use accurate data. Many students write body paragraphs that are just random lists of numbers. Today I'll show you how to do it the right way so your response actually flows and makes sense.

What Are Body Paragraphs For?

After your introduction and overview, the body paragraphs are where you explain the details. You usually need two body paragraphs. Their job is to support the overview by giving specific information and examples from the chart.

Think of it like telling a story: the overview gives the big picture, and the body paragraphs fill in the important details.

How to Structure Your Body Paragraphs

Don’t try to describe everything in one paragraph. Split the information logically.

1
Body Paragraph 1: Describe the most noticeable or important trends/features (usually the biggest changes or highest/lowest points).
2
Body Paragraph 2: Cover the remaining information, make comparisons, and talk about other interesting patterns.

Golden Rule: Group similar information together. Never jump randomly between different parts of the chart.

How to Describe Trends Effectively

Good body paragraphs focus on what changed, when it changed, and how much it changed.

In the first quarter, the sales rose from 50 units to 120 units. This upward trend continued until June when figures reached a peak of 250 units. However, after that, sales dropped sharply to only 80 units by the end of the year.

Using Data Accurately

You must include numbers, but don’t list every single one. Choose the most important ones and use approximations when possible.

  • Use exact numbers when they are key (e.g. "exactly 50%")
  • Use approximations for less important figures (e.g. "around 45%", "approximately 200 units", "just over 1,000")
  • Always mention units (%, million, tons, etc.)

Weak Example: It went from 12 to 45 to 67 to 89.

Better Example: Sales rose significantly from 12,000 in 2018 to 89,000 in 2022, with the biggest increase happening between 2020 and 2021.

Making Comparisons

Comparison is very important in Task 1. Look for things that are similar or very different.

Useful Comparison Phrases:
• significantly higher than • twice as much as • the same as • while …, … • in contrast • compared to • whereas

Example: While consumption in the USA remained relatively stable, China’s figure increased dramatically and overtook the USA in 2019.

Useful Vocabulary for Body Paragraphs

Describing Increase:
rose sharply • increased significantly • climbed steadily • surged • went up dramatically
Describing Decrease:
fell sharply • declined steadily • dropped suddenly • plummeted • decreased gradually
Fluctuation & Stability:
fluctuated • varied • remained stable • leveled off • stayed constant
Time Expressions:
between 2015 and 2020 • over the next five years • from then on • during this period • by the end of

Common Mistakes in Body Paragraphs

  • Listing every number without any grouping or trend description
  • Repeating the same words again and again (rose, rose, rose…)
  • Not making comparisons when the chart clearly shows them
  • Including personal opinions (“This is bad for the economy”)
  • Writing too short (less than 2 good body paragraphs)
  • Using wrong tenses (use past tense for old data, present for general facts)

Practical Tips for Better Body Paragraphs

  1. After writing your overview, look at the chart again and decide the best way to split the information into two paragraphs
  2. Highlight the key numbers you want to mention before writing
  3. Try to use a mix of simple and complex sentences
  4. Always check that your data is accurate. Small mistakes can cost marks!
  5. Practice describing only the body paragraphs for different charts (without full essays)
  6. Read your paragraphs aloud. They should flow naturally

The body paragraphs are your chance to prove you can analyze and describe data properly. Focus on clarity, accuracy, and variety in your language. Keep practicing and you’ll see your scores improve fast.
