Writing Conclusions

How to write impactful essay conclusions

Hey! We've covered introductions and body paragraphs and now it's time to talk about the conclusion. Many students rush through the conclusion or just repeat what they already said. But a strong conclusion leaves a lasting impression on the examiner and can really boost your Task Response and Coherence scores. Let me show you how to write a powerful, natural-sounding conclusion that ties everything together nicely.

Why the Conclusion Matters

The conclusion is your final chance to show the examiner that you have a clear, well-thought-out response to the question. It should feel complete and satisfying. A weak or missing conclusion often stops students from reaching Band 7+, even if the rest of the essay is good.

What Should a Good Conclusion Include?

A strong conclusion should:

  • Summarize your main points (without repeating the same sentences)
  • Restate your opinion or position clearly
  • Give a final thought, recommendation, or prediction (if suitable)
  • Be 30–50 words long (usually 2–3 sentences)

Important: Never introduce new ideas or examples in the conclusion. This is the time to wrap up, not continue the discussion.

Simple & Effective Conclusion Formula

1
Start with a summary phrase like “In conclusion,” or “To sum up,”
2
Restate your main position and key reasons briefly.
3
End with a final comment, suggestion, or broader implication.

Examples

Question: Some people believe that the best way to increase road safety is to increase the minimum legal age for driving. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Weak Conclusion:

In conclusion, I agree with this idea. It is important for road safety.

Strong Conclusion (Band 7.5+):

In conclusion, raising the minimum legal age for driving and riding motorbikes is an effective way to improve road safety. Younger drivers are often more impulsive and less experienced, which leads to more accidents. Therefore, governments should seriously consider this measure to make our roads safer for everyone.

Useful Phrases for Conclusions

Starting the Conclusion:
In conclusion, • To conclude, • Overall, • Taking everything into consideration,
Restating Position:
It is clear that… • This essay has shown that… • I firmly believe that…
Final Comment:
It is recommended that… • This issue should be addressed by… • Only then can we…

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Simply repeating the introduction word for word
  • Introducing new arguments or examples
  • Making the conclusion too short (only one sentence)
  • Ending suddenly without a proper closing sentence
  • Using informal language or contractions
  • Being too repetitive with vocabulary

Practical Tips for Better Conclusions

  1. Write the conclusion after you've finished the body paragraphs. It's easier when you know exactly what you discussed
  2. Read your introduction and conclusion together. They should feel connected but not identical
  3. Keep it balanced! Do NOT make it too long or too short!
  4. Practice writing conclusions for different essay types
  5. Always check that your final opinion matches what you wrote in the introduction

A solid conclusion gives your essay a sense of completion and professionalism. It shows the examiner that you can organize your thoughts and finish strongly. Spend time practicing this part. It is often what separates good essays from excellent ones.
