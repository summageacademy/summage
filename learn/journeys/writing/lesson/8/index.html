Writing Strong Body Paragraphs

Topic sentences, explanations and examples

Hey! If the introduction is the face of your essay, the body paragraphs are its heart and soul. This is where you prove your point, develop your ideas, and convince the examiner that your opinion makes sense. Most students write body paragraphs that are too short, too vague, or just list ideas without explanation. In this lesson, I’ll show you how to write strong, well-developed body paragraphs that can push your score to Band 7.5 or higher.

Why Body Paragraphs Are So Important

Body paragraphs make up the biggest part of your essay. This is where you show depth of thinking, clear reasoning, and good language control. Examiners look closely at whether your ideas are relevant, well-explained, and supported with examples. Weak body paragraphs are one of the fastest ways to stay stuck at Band 6.

The Perfect Structure for a Strong Body Paragraph

1
Topic Sentence
Clearly state the main idea of the paragraph.
2
Explanation
Explain why this idea is true or important.
3
Example / Evidence
Give a real-life example or specific detail to support your point.
4
Link / Conclusion (optional)
Connect back to the main topic or lead to the next paragraph.

Example of a Strong Body Paragraph

Question: Many people believe that social media has more negative effects than positive ones. Do you agree or disagree?

One major reason why social media can be harmful is that it often leads to increased anxiety and low self-esteem among young people. Constantly comparing themselves to others’ highlight reels makes teenagers feel inadequate and unhappy with their own lives. For example, a study by the University of Cambridge found that young users who spent more than two hours daily on platforms like Instagram reported significantly higher levels of anxiety. This clearly shows how social media can damage mental health if not used responsibly.

Useful Techniques to Develop Ideas

  • Explain why – Don’t just state an idea, tell the reader why it matters.
  • Give causes or results – Show the reasons behind something or its consequences.
  • Use examples – Real-world examples, personal experiences, or studies make your argument stronger.
  • Make comparisons – Compare past vs present or different situations.

Linking Words & Phrases for Body Paragraphs

Starting a Paragraph:
First of all… • Another key reason is… • Furthermore… • In addition…
Explaining:
This is because… • The main reason is that… • As a result… • This leads to…
Giving Examples:
For instance… • For example… • A good example of this is… • Take… as an example

Common Mistakes Students Make

  • Writing only 1 or 2 sentences per paragraph
  • Having no clear topic sentence
  • Listing ideas without explaining them
  • Using the same simple linking words repeatedly (and, but, so)
  • Including irrelevant or off-topic information
  • Not giving any examples to support their opinion

Practical Tips for Writing Strong Body Paragraphs

  1. Decide your two main ideas during the planning stage
  2. Make sure each paragraph focuses on only one main idea
  3. Always ask yourself “Why?” and “How?” after stating your point
  4. Try to include at least one clear example in each body paragraph
  5. Use a mix of simple and complex sentences
  6. After writing, check if each paragraph clearly supports your overall opinion

Strong body paragraphs are what separate good essays from great ones. They show the examiner that you can think critically and express ideas clearly. Practice writing just the body paragraphs for different topics. It is one of the best ways to improve fast.
