Writing a Strong Introduction

How to write clear and accurate introductions for Task 1

Hey! Now that you understand the big picture of IELTS Writing Task 1, let's focus on the very first thing the examiner reads, your introduction! A strong introduction sets a great first impression and helps you score higher in Task Achievement and Lexical Resource.

Why is the Introduction Important?

The introduction is your chance to show the examiner that you understand the task and can use your own words. It should be short (1-2 sentences) but clear. A weak or copied introduction can immediately lower your score.

The Golden Rule for Task 1 Introduction

Paraphrase the question.

Instead of copying the exact words from the task, rewrite them using synonyms and different sentence structure.

Remember: Never copy the full sentence from the question. Examiners are trained to spot this and it hurts your score.

Simple Formula for a Strong Introduction

1
Start with: "The [chart / graph / table / map / process] shows / illustrates / presents ..."
2
Add key information: what is being shown, where, and over what time period (if relevant).

Examples of Good Introductions

Original Task: The graph below shows the number of tourists visiting a Caribbean island between 2010 and 2017.

The chart illustrates the number of visitors to a Caribbean island from 2010 to 2017.

Another Example:

The bar chart compares the amount of electricity produced by different energy sources in Germany in the year 2022.

For Maps:

The maps show how a small town developed between 1990 and the present day.

Useful Paraphrasing Vocabulary

Shows / Illustrates / Presents / Depicts / Represents
Information about / Data on / Figures for
Changes in / Development of / Comparison of
Between 2010 and 2020 / Over a 10-year period / From 2015 to 2025

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Copying the exact words from the question
  • Making the introduction too long (more than 2 sentences)
  • Adding personal opinions or predictions
  • Writing only “This chart shows…” without any details
  • Using very basic vocabulary (“The picture shows…”)

Quick Tips for Strong Introductions

  1. Spend 30–40 seconds planning your paraphrase
  2. Always mention the type of visual (graph, chart, table, etc.)
  3. Include the main subject and time period if given
  4. Keep it simple and accurate
  5. Practice paraphrasing 5 different questions every day

A good introduction is like a strong handshake — it creates a positive impression right from the start. Master this and you’re already on your way to a higher band score.
