TOEFL vs IELTS 2026: Which One Should You Take and Why?

Complete Comparison Guide for English Learners

Choosing between TOEFL and IELTS can feel overwhelming for many English learners. Both tests prove your English proficiency for university admissions, visas or jobs. However, the right choice can save you time and lots of money. In 2026, the decision is even more important due to major updates in the TOEFL iBT. This blog post is designed to help you decide.

Quick Overview

TOEFL iBT

Administered by ETS, it's fully computer-based with a strong focus on academic English, especially North American style. Since January 21, 2026, the test is much shorter (around 90-120 minutes) and uses a new 1-6 scoring scale (aligned with CEFR levels) with adaptive elements.

IELTS Academic

Managed by British Council, IDP and Cambridge. It offers computer or paper-based options. Also, It must be noted that many centers shifting to computer in 2026. The test takes about 2 hours 45 minutes and includes a face-to-face Speaking section with the familiar 0-9 band score.

Key Differences in 2026

Here's a side-by-side comparison to make things clearer:

Duration

TOEFL: ~90-120 minutes
IELTS: ~2 hours 45 minutes

Speaking Section

TOEFL: Computer-recorded responses
IELTS: Face-to-face with an examiner

Scoring

TOEFL: New 1-6 scale
IELTS: 0-9 bands

English Style

TOEFL: American academic English
IELTS: British/International English

Pros and Cons

TOEFL Pros

Shorter test means less fatigue. Fully computer-based suits fast typists. Excellent for US and Canada university applications. Adaptive format feels more modern, too.

TOEFL Cons

Computer-based Speaking can feel unnatural. New format requires updated preparation materials.

IELTS Pros

Face-to-face Speaking helps conversational learners. More flexible scheduling and Strong acceptance in UK, Australia and for immigration.

IELTS Cons

Longer duration. Speaking interview can be stressful for introverts.

Which One Should You Take?

Choose TOEFL if:

• You're applying to universities in the US or Canada
• You prefer typing and computer interfaces
• You want a quicker test experience

Choose IELTS if:

• You're targeting UK, Australia or Europe
• You perform better in live conversations
• You prefer a more traditional test format

Pro Tip: Always check the specific requirements of your target universities or visa programs first. Many accept both, but score expectations may differ.

Final Advice

Neither test is universally easier. It mostly depends on your personal strengths. Take free practice tests for both (available on official ETS and British Council websites) to see which format feels more comfortable for you.

You can also check out our IELTS-preparation tools to practice and enhance your skills.

Focus on consistent daily practice such as listening, reading, speaking and writing. The most important thing is starting early and choosing the test that plays to your strengths. A good score will open many doors!
